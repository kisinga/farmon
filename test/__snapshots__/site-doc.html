KC868-A16

Network & Recovery

The KC868-A16 supports both Ethernet (LAN8720 PHY) and WiFi. ESPHome enforces XOR — exactly one transport is active per device. Pick one in MajiFlow's Connection panel; the choice is baked into the firmware at flash time.

Ethernet WiFi
Default for this board ✓
Speed / reliability Wired, deterministic Depends on RF environment
Credentials needed None SSID + password
Diagnostic sensor ethernet_info (IP) wifi_info (IP, SSID, MAC) + wifi_signal (dBm)
Static IP support ✓ via Connection → IP Configuration ✓ same panel
Recovery if the network goes down None — replug the cable, or direct-connect to a laptop (see below) Improv or join the fallback AP

Where the dashboard lives

The entity dashboard only serves on the device's station IP — i.e. the IP it gets from your home network (DHCP) or whatever you set as manual_ip. It does not serve at 192.168.4.1 (the fallback-AP gateway address). This is an ESPHome limitation: web_server doesn't bind the SoftAP interface (esphome/issues#4333), so when wifi is down there is no entity-control surface.

To control the device, it must be reachable on a network — either the home wifi, or via ethernet.

Recovery on WiFi transport

When the configured wifi credentials stop working (network changed, password rotated, etc.), use one of these to push new credentials. Neither restores the dashboard until wifi rejoins.

1. Improv Wi-Fi — recommended. Open standard from the Home Assistant ecosystem (open-source, github.com/improv-wifi). Two flavours, both compiled in:

  • BLE (esp32_improv): pair from any Chromium-based browser at improv-wifi.com (or the ESPHome / HA companion app). The page uses WebBluetooth to talk directly to the device — no server roundtrip, no account.
  • Serial (improv_serial): same page, but over USB via WebSerial. Useful when BLE is unreachable or the device is bench-bound.

2. Fallback AP + captive portal — works without a Chromium browser, but degraded UX.

  • After ~90 s of failed wifi association, the device broadcasts <friendly_name> Fallback (password = your wifi password).
  • Joining the AP triggers your phone/laptop's captive-portal popup automatically.
  • Use that popup's Configure WiFi form to enter new credentials.
  • On ESPHome 2025.2.0+, the popup may render blank. If so, the credential-save endpoint still works — paste your new SSID/password using the form fields the OS shows, or use Improv instead.
  • The 192.168.4.1 address itself shows no useful page; don't bother typing it directly.

Recovery on Ethernet transport

If the cable drops the device is unreachable until it's replugged. There's no on-device recovery surface for ethernet (no AP, no Improv-over-cable).

Direct-to-laptop debug (no router)

Useful for bench testing. Plug an ethernet cable from your laptop straight into the KC868-A16 (the LAN8720 PHY does auto-MDIX). On the laptop:

  1. Set the wired interface to a manual IP in the same subnet as the device — e.g. device 192.168.150.142, laptop 192.168.150.100, mask 255.255.255.0.
  2. Open http://<device-static-ip>/ — full dashboard, no router involved.

Requires a static IP on the device (set in Connection → IP Configuration).

Web dashboard

Always emitted on port 80. Reachable at the device's IP (home-network or direct-cable). Every relay, sensor, switch, and number entity declared in the topology is exposed for manual control — nothing extra to configure.

Power Requirements

The KC868-A16 board, every 12 V solenoid valve, and every 4–20 mA / 0–10 V sensor draw from one 12 V DC supply wired to the green DC input terminal block.

Load Typical Notes
Board idle (Ethernet up, OLED off) ~120 mA @ 12 V Datasheet figure
All 16 relay coils energized +320 mA @ 12 V Worst-case transient — never sustained in practice
24 V solenoid valves external supply Use the dry contact across the relay; do not wire 24 V into the board
4–20 mA sensor (loop powered) ~25 mA @ 12 V each Series with sensor + 250 Ω burden
0–10 V sensor ~10 mA @ 12 V each Through the 100 kΩ divider to the ADC pin

Sizing rule of thumb: 0.15 A × controllers + 0.5 A × valves on simultaneously + 0.05 A × sensors, then add 30% headroom. A 5 A regulated 12 V brick is comfortable for the largest single-board deployment.

Brown-out: the ESP32 brown-out detector trips around 2.7 V on the 3.3 V rail. If you see random reboots when relays click in, the supply is sagging — either too small or the wiring is too thin (use ≥ 0.75 mm² for runs longer than 1 m).

Fuse: a 5 A inline fuse on the +12 V input is mandatory if your supply is unfused.

Pin Architecture

The KC868-A16 has segregated pin pools — unlike boards with general-purpose GPIOs, each pin pool serves a specific function and cannot be reassigned.

Pool Pins Count Capability Used For
Relay outputs OUT1–OUT16 16 digital (via PCF8574) Valves (2 pins each), pumps (1 pin each)
ADC inputs GPIO36, GPIO34, GPIO35, GPIO39 4 digital, adc Tank level sensors (0–5V, op-amp conditioned)
Sensor headers GPIO32, GPIO33, GPIO14 3 digital, pulse_counter Flow sensors, DS18B20 temperature probes
Digital inputs IN1–IN16 16 digital (via PCF8574) Future: float switches, dry contacts
RS485 GPIO13 (TX), GPIO16 (RX) 2 Reserved VFD/Modbus devices (MAX13487E transceiver)
I2C bus GPIO4 (SDA), GPIO5 (SCL) 2 Reserved Internal: PCF8574 expander communication
Ethernet GPIO23 (MDC), GPIO18 (MDIO), GPIO17 (CLK) 3 Reserved LAN8720 PHY

Total exposed: 39 user-assignable pins (16 OUT + 16 IN + 4 ADC + 3 sensor headers). Total reserved: 7 pins (I2C + Ethernet + RS485 are auto-reserved by MajiFlow).

Boot-strapping pins (avoid for inputs)

GPIO0, GPIO2, GPIO12, GPIO15 are ESP32 strapping pins. The board package marks them reserved — assigning a topology entity to one surfaces a validation error before flash.

Polarity

All onboard relays are active-low (the PCF8574 sinks current to energize the coil). MajiFlow defaults to Active-low polarity on entity wiring — keep that unless you've modified the board.

On-Board Peripherals

I²C bus & expanders

The KC868-A16 reaches its 16 relays + 16 inputs through four PCF8574 I²C GPIO expanders behind a single onboard I²C bus on GPIO4 (SDA) / GPIO5 (SCL). Pull-ups are populated on the board — you don't need to add your own on the board's bus.

Address Role Pin labels
0x24 Relay outputs 1–8 OUT1…OUT8 (active-low coil drivers)
0x25 Relay outputs 9–16 OUT9…OUT16
0x21 Digital inputs 1–8 IN1…IN8 (optocoupled, pull to GND to assert)
0x22 Digital inputs 9–16 IN9…IN16

External I²C devices (extra expanders, OLEDs, sensors): bring SDA/SCL out of the J6 header. Stay under the standard fast-mode budget — total bus capacitance < 400 pF, total length under ~1 m for reliable 100 kHz.

Address conflicts: never assign a new device to 0x21, 0x22, 0x24, 0x25.

Sensor headers (3× analog)

J3, J4, J5 are 3-pin headers (signal, +12 V, GND) wired to GPIO32, GPIO33, GPIO35 through a 100 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider. Inputs accept 0–12 V, scaled into the ESP32's 0–3.3 V ADC.

  • Use J3 (GPIO32) and J4 (GPIO33) for pulse counters (flow meters) — these GPIOs support pulse counter peripheral. GPIO35 is ADC-only.
  • Calibration is done in the MajiFlow level-sensor entity — exposes number.…_cal_empty / number.…_cal_full HA entities.

RS485 / Modbus (MAX13487E transceiver)

GPIO13 (TX), GPIO16 (RX) are reserved for RS485 via the onboard half-duplex transceiver. Direction is auto-controlled — no DE/RE pin needed.

  • A/B terminals on the green DC block. Daisy-chain to VFDs / Modbus sensors. Terminate with 120 Ω at each end of the trunk.
  • Common ground between board and Modbus device is required if they don't share the same power supply.

Realistic configurations

Sizing tiers below are agnostic to the application — a small irrigation plot, a hotel rooftop tank set, a greenhouse zone head, and a small distribution manifold all map to the same controller footprint when the valve / tank / flow-sensor counts match.

Compact (e.g. small plot, single rooftop, one-zone greenhouse): 1 pump, 3 valves, 2 tanks, 1 flow sensor — uses 8/16 OUT, 2/4 ADC, 1/3 pulse counter. Room to grow.

Medium (e.g. multi-zone irrigation, hotel back-of-house ring, mid-size greenhouse): 1 pump, 6 valves, 4 tanks, 3 flow sensors — uses 14/16 OUT, 4/4 ADC, 3/3 pulse counter. Near maximum: 1 spare OUT pin.

Maxed out (every pin utilized): 1 pump, 7 valves, 4 tanks, 3 flow sensors — uses 15/16 OUT, 4/4 ADC, 3/3 pulse counter. No spare capacity on any pool.

Beyond one controller: for installations exceeding these limits, deploy multiple KC868-A16 controllers and connect them via MajiFlow's multi-system site feature with inter-system links.

ADC Voltage Range

The KC868-A16's 4 ADC inputs accept 0–5 V from external sensors. An onboard LM324 op-amp scales this to 0–3.3 V for the ESP32's ADC.

Calibration in MajiFlow: the Cal Empty V and Cal Full V values represent the ESP32-side voltage (0–3.3 V range), not the original sensor output (0–5 V). Calibrate by:

  1. Drain the tank to empty → note the raw voltage reading in Home Assistant.
  2. Fill the tank to full → note the raw voltage reading.
  3. Enter these values as Cal Empty and Cal Full.

The Raw Voltage diagnostic sensor is exposed to HA to assist with calibration.

Digital Inputs

The 16 optocoupled digital inputs (IN1–IN16) accept 6–24 V signals and are isolated from the ESP32 — ground loops on field wiring won't reach the MCU. Pull-down to GND via the field signal asserts the input.

These pins are currently reserved for future entity types:

  • Float switch / dry contact binary sensors
  • External trigger inputs
  • Alarm / fault inputs from external equipment

The pins are defined in the board YAML with caps: [digital] and ready for use when the corresponding entity types ship in MajiFlow.

Wiring tip: use ≥ 1 kΩ in series with the field signal to clamp the optocoupler current. The board's onboard resistor handles up to 24 V cleanly; higher voltages will saturate the LED.

The KC868-A16 self-test firmware exercises every onboard subsystem in one bench cycle. Probes that don't apply (OLED, LoRa, WiFi-only diagnostics, battery) are excluded automatically by the codegen.

Tank-to-Tank Overflow Protection

  1. Pre-start check: dest tank > 95% → reject start
  2. Per-route max_runtime_s: sized to tank capacity (hard fault if exceeded)
  3. Hardware float switch (REQUIRED): high-level switch on every destination tank — independent of ESP32 software

Sensor Fault Detection

Each flow sensor has built-in fault detection. When the pump is RUNNING and flow was previously confirmed above the configured flow threshold, 3 consecutive below-threshold readings trigger a sensor_fault binary sensor in HA. This is distinct from no_flow (never established) — sensor fault means flow was confirmed then the sensor stopped reporting.

Installation

For installers and electricians at the job site — sensor placement, valve mounting, tank-sensor calibration, and PSU + cable specs. Board-specific power and pinout details live under Device Reference; this chapter covers the field side of the wiring.

Installation Guidelines

Flow Sensors

Placement — The 10D/5D Rule

Flow sensors require straight, undisturbed pipe upstream and downstream for accurate readings:

  • 10D upstream: 10 pipe diameters of straight pipe before the sensor
  • 5D downstream: 5 pipe diameters of straight pipe after the sensor

For a 1" (25mm) pipe: 250mm straight before, 125mm straight after.

Float Valve Requirement

A float valve must be installed upstream of each flow sensor on routes filling a destination tank. When the tank is full, the float valve closes and flow drops to zero — this is how MajiFlow detects tank-full and triggers a clean stop via the flow watchdog.

Mounting & Electrical

  • Mount horizontally with the impeller axis vertical; arrow matches flow direction
  • Use shielded cable for runs > 2m · Pull-up resistor (10kΩ to 3.3V) recommended

Valves

  • Mount horizontally or with the actuator above the pipe
  • Default travel time: 15 seconds
  • 2-wire control: one wire for OPEN, one for CLOSE. Never energize both simultaneously — ESPHome interlocks prevent this
  • Wire gauge: 18 AWG minimum for runs up to 15m; 16 AWG for longer runs

Tank Level Sensors

Analog pressure sensors at the tank bottom measure water column height. Mount at the lowest point of the tank.

Calibrate via Home Assistant: set Cal Empty (V) and Cal Full (V) number entities.

Tank readings are suppressed during pump operation to avoid pressure artifacts.

General Notes

  • Pipe sizing: Match pipe diameter to expected flow rates
  • Pressure regulators: Install upstream if mains pressure exceeds 6 bar
  • Check valves: Non-return valves on tank inlet lines
  • Strainers: Install upstream of flow sensors to prevent debris fouling
  • Float switches: Required on every destination tank as hardware safety interlock

DC power supply (12 V rail)

The KC868-A16 board, every 12 V DC solenoid valve, and every sensor draw from one 12 V supply.

Sizing

MajiFlow's default valve type is a 2-wire motorized valve: the controller energizes an OPEN or CLOSE motor for ~15 s of travel, then the valve mechanically latches and draws zero current. Steady-state load is essentially just the board.

Concurrent valve activations are firmware-capped at 2 (briefly 3 during a route handoff). With motorized valves, worst-case demand is short and modest:

Installation Steady (no valves moving) Peak (handoff: pump on + 2 motors traveling)
Any size, 1–8 valves ~0.25 A ~1.0 A for ~15 s

One-line rule: A 12 V / 2 A regulated supply (24 W) covers any single-controller install with motorized valves. Direct-wired DC solenoid valves are different — each holds ~0.6 A continuously while open, so an install with those needs 12 V / 5 A (60 W) instead.

Per-device current budget (for the curious)

Use a regulated supply (SMPS), not an unregulated wall-wart. Motor start and any solenoid inrush will sag an unregulated 12 V brick below the ESP32's brown-out threshold.

DC cable runs

For 12 V DC, voltage drop is the binding constraint — not current capacity. A 12 V solenoid pulls in at ≈ 10.2 V (85 % of nominal); during inrush, end-of-cable voltage must stay above this.

All lengths assume copper conductor at 20 °C, one-way distance from controller to load, sized for the inrush moment (the worst case).

Mains and the pump (separate from the DC rail)

The KC868-A16 relay does not carry pump current. It switches a contactor coil; the contactor switches the pump. This is true even for small pumps where the relay rating is technically sufficient — the contactor is the field-replaceable wear part.

Contactor / VFD threshold

Pump size Switching Why
≤ 0.5 HP Relay direct permitted, contactor still preferred LRA ~12–15 A, within 10 A relay rating but degrading
> 0.5 HP Contactor required LRA exceeds relay rating
≥ 1.5 HP Contactor + soft-starter, or VFD DOL inrush stresses pump and supply
≥ 2 HP, or any 3φ VFD via RS485/Modbus — no relay Outside the relay path entirely

Cable size assumes ≤ 20 m run. For longer runs, step up one size, or compute voltage drop (> 3 % at FLA = upsize).

Notes:

  • D-curve breakers tolerate motor inrush without nuisance-tripping; C-curve is fine up to ~1 HP.
  • Submersible-pump nameplates often understate LRA — use the upper end of the inrush column for sizing.
  • A VFD or soft-starter collapses inrush to ~1.2–1.5 × FLA, making the LRA column irrelevant.

Always include

  • Thermal overload alongside the contactor — protects the pump motor.
  • Dry-run / no-load protection on the pump itself — saves submersibles.
  • Earth bonding of pump body, contactor enclosure, and controller DIN rail.

Common mistakes

  • Sharing the pump's earth return through the DC rail. Don't. Star-ground separately at a single bonding point.
  • Daisy-chaining valve power. Each valve gets its own pair from the controller — daisy-chained valves brown each other out during inrush.
  • Skipping the contactor "because the relay is rated 10 A". Inductive inrush is what kills relays; welded contacts mean the pump won't stop on command.
  • Running sensor cables alongside mains in the same conduit. Induced noise wrecks pulse counts. Keep ≥ 10 cm separation, or use separate conduit.
  • Unregulated 12 V wall-warts. They sag under solenoid inrush and brown-out the ESP32. Use a regulated SMPS.
